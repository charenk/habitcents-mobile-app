# HowItWorksSheet (components/today/HowItWorksSheet.tsx)

## Direction (current)
How skips and habits work, told on request. A decision-style sheet: serif title, then one connected spine running the length of the sheet through five rows under two phase headings, a tinted long-arc block, and one secondary "Got it". The spine's dots are outlined grey while we are describing what the app found and solid sage from the moment the user takes one on, and the phase heading between them, "Then you break it", is where the colour changes. Each row leads with one locked word in bold and defines it in the same sentence. Opened from the underlined link on Today's Kept true-zero state, and from nowhere else. It carries the only explanation of the kept mechanic a user with no data can reach.

## States
Closed; open (Sheet's enter); closing (Sheet's exit, reduced-motion path inherited). No state of its own: nothing here mutates.

## Decisions
- 2026-09-10 (Charen): **the leak becomes the habit, and the sheet shows where.** Two phase headings, "First we find it" and "Then you break it", and a row for the step that was missing entirely: you pick one. Why: the app already draws this line (`LEAKS FOUND`, then `BREAKING NOW`, and the dock says "Break another habit"), but row 3 said "Break a leak, and every skip keeps...", using the pre-commitment word for a post-commitment act. With no moment of handover, a skip belonged to nothing, which is exactly the relationship Charen said was missing. Five rows now, up from four. **Supersedes the four-row decision below.**
- 2026-09-10: **one spine instead of four glyphs.** A single connector runs through every row, phase headings included, and changes colour at the handover. Why: the relationship between the words is the content, so it should be drawn rather than described; a column of unrelated icons said nothing about order or dependency. The dots carry the same outlined-versus-solid signal the tab bar uses. The Pencil / Sprout / Check / Repeat set is retired with the rows it labelled.
- 2026-09-10: **each row is one locked word in bold plus its definition**, in one sentence. Why: leak, habit, skip, slip and kept are the app's whole vocabulary and this is the only surface that can define them; naming them inline teaches the words without a glossary. Slip rides inside the skip row rather than taking a node of its own, because a node implied it moves something, which is the one thing it never does.
- 2026-09-10 (Charen): **the long arc closes the sheet, money before the number.** "The money is yours from the first skip. Rewiring the habit is slower, around 66 skips, and that first one counts toward it too." Why: Charen's objection was that it read as a long process before anything changes, and that is false, `utils/habitLogging.ts` `keptOnDay` adds on the first skip. Leading with that answers the objection by ordering rather than by deletion. The number stays because it is the app's one real anchor and matches `arcSupportLine` on habit detail. Rejected, and it was the earlier recommendation: replacing 66 with a four-stage track using LongArc's chapter names, because nothing on this sheet drives that track, so a lit "Deciding" segment would be an invented statistic. Also rejected: cutting the arc entirely, which would leave the sheet explaining a money mechanic and never saying the habit itself changes.
- 2026-09-10: `strings.today.howItWorksRows` becomes `howItWorksFindRows` / `howItWorksBreakRows`, each row a `{ term, rest }` pair. The pair carries a documented translation escape hatch: a language that cannot lead with the term puts the whole sentence in `rest` and leaves `term` empty, and the row renders unemphasized rather than forcing English word order. Written that way deliberately because the localization worker is live on `constants/strings.ts` and `today` is the section it is gated on (decision 8 on the routine status board).
- 2026-09-10: **the sheet scrolls, capped at 86 percent of the window**, the pattern PickOneSheet's gated body already uses. Why: Sheet has no scroll of its own and this copy is uncapped under Dynamic Type on purpose (it is content, like EmptyState's body). At `accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large` the five rows plus the arc block run past a 932pt screen and took the serif title and "Got it" off it entirely, so neither was reachable. This was carried as an Open item before today; adding a row and a block turned it into a blocker. Caught on the simulator, not by a test.
- 2026-09-07 (Charen): the explanation lives here, behind a link, not on the pane. Why: explanatory prose does not belong in the empty-state pattern; the pane keeps its silhouette and the telling is one tap away for whoever wants it. See [EmptyState](EmptyState.md) and today.md for the reversal of ADR 0039.
- 2026-09-07: this revives the "how-it-works" sheet the redesign retired (`design/redesign-handoff/03-onboarding.md`, three icon rows and an OK) rather than inventing a new surface. Its third row survived the retirement as onboarding's `outcomeKeptCounts`; the shape was already proven.
- 2026-09-07: four rows, not three. **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-10 by the five-row spine above; the fact-checking below still binds the copy.** The ADR 0039 lines (log, around four logs at one place, a skip keeps) plus "A slip records what happened; it never takes anything back." Why: the question is how skips and habits are measured, and a slip is the half people worry about. Verified against the code before writing: `contexts/HabitsContext.tsx` leaves `goal.kept` unchanged on a slipped answer and adds partial-slip credit, `utils/habitLogging.ts` `keptOnDay` only ever adds, and the one subtraction (`changeTodayAnswer`) corrects a previously recorded skip. The ADR 0039 guardrails still bind the copy: no rate, no total, no "the one you buy most often".
- 2026-09-07: glyphs Pencil (log; SquarePen means edit-a-row elsewhere), Sprout (a leak surfaces; InfoRibbon's growth mark), Check (a skip; the check-in's own mark), Repeat (a slip; the habit ran again). Rejected: Minus for the slip, which reads as "subtract", the one thing the row denies. **RETIRED 2026-09-10: the sheet has no glyphs; the spine replaced them.**
- 2026-09-07: a bottom "Got it" (secondary). Why: decision sheets keep bottom CTAs; ADR 0031's "no in-sheet Cancel" is about form sheets, and an acknowledgement is not a cancel. Handle and scrim dismiss too.
- 2026-09-07: each row is one VoiceOver stop carrying the whole line; the glyph is decorative. Same idiom the removed steps list used.
- 2026-09-07: opening fires `how_it_works_opened`, no payload. Why: the only way to learn whether a deliberately quiet link gets found at all. Approved by Charen (analytics contracts are human-gated, ADR 0035).

## Open
- The arc block's `Text` originally reused the row style, which carries `flex: 1` so it can share a flex row with the rail, and that collapsed the block to one clipped line. Caught on the simulator, not in tests: nothing asserts rendered height. Any future block outside a spine row needs its own text style.
- Reachable only from Kept true zero. Kept Quiet shows the detection meter instead; whether a returning user ever wants this sheet again (from habit detail, say) is a separate question.
- Closed 2026-09-10: body lines stay uncapped under Dynamic Type, and the sheet now scrolls under an 86 percent cap rather than overflowing. Verified at `accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large` on the iPhone 16 Plus, title and button both reachable. A shorter device (SE) is still owed a look.
- The scroll is a plain ScrollView with no fade at its end, so on a short screen the copy cuts at the cap with nothing marking that there is more. ScrollFade exists for exactly this and is used only on Today; whether sheets should take it is a question for drawers.md, not this file.
